# Experiment 02 — Multi-Step Open-Loop Prediction

## Objective

Evaluate how prediction errors accumulate when a neural world model recursively predicts future Pendulum states without receiving intermediate ground-truth observations.

## Experimental Setup

- Environment: Gymnasium Pendulum-v1
- Model: MLP with two hidden layers of 128 ReLU units
- Training objective: normalized one-step state-delta prediction
- Evaluation: 10 independently initialized trajectories
- Prediction horizon: 100 simulation steps (5 seconds)
- Actions: identical random torque sequences applied to both the environment and the learned model
- Metric: mean squared error across the three state components, averaged over evaluation trajectories

## Results

| Prediction step | Mean state MSE |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 0.000012 |
| 5 | 0.000564 |
| 10 | 0.004254 |
| 20 | 0.009564 |
| 50 | 2.329447 |
| 100 | 6.113632 |

## Observations

The learned model produces accurate short-horizon predictions, but its open-loop accuracy deteriorates substantially over longer horizons.

In the visualized example, the predicted angular velocity initially follows the true trajectory closely. After approximately 30–50 simulation steps, the trajectories increasingly diverge. By the end of the rollout, the predicted and actual angular velocities differ substantially, including in their direction.

## Interpretation

Potential contributors to the observed divergence include:

1. Accumulation of local prediction errors during recursive rollout.
2. Distribution shift caused by using predicted states as subsequent inputs.
3. Lack of explicit enforcement of the geometric constraint cos²(θ) + sin²(θ) = 1.
4. Training exclusively with a one-step prediction objective.

These factors are plausible explanations rather than individually verified causes.

## Limitations

- Evaluation covers only 10 trajectories.
- The reported aggregate MSE combines state variables with different physical scales.
- No per-state-variable error statistics or uncertainty estimates are reported.
- The model was evaluated on random action sequences rather than trajectories generated by a planning algorithm.
- Multi-step prediction accuracy does not directly establish closed-loop control performance.
